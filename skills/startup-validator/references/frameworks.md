# Framework Reference

> Use these frameworks as lenses, not as a ritual checklist. Score the idea only after tagging evidence quality.

## 1. Thiel 7 Questions: venture-scale lens

Use these to judge whether the idea can become a meaningful startup, not merely a useful feature.

| Question | What to test | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering | Can the solution be 10x better on a metric customers care about? | incremental improvement with no switching reason |
| Timing | Why now? What changed in technology, regulation, behavior, cost, or distribution? | right idea too early/late |
| Monopoly | Can it start in a small market and become dominant there? | broad market with no wedge |
| People | Does the team have founder-market fit and credibility? | generic team chasing trend |
| Distribution | Can the company reach buyers repeatedly and affordably? | product obsession with no channel |
| Durability | What protects it over 10+ years? | easy clone or platform dependency |
| Secret | What non-obvious truth does the team know? | consensus idea with no insight |

## 2. YC / Paul Graham lenses

Key questions:

- Who wants this urgently right now?
- Is this a problem the founder has personally noticed or lived near?
- Is there a small, reachable wedge?
- Can the team do things that do not scale to learn and delight early users?
- Are users pulling the product from the company, or is the company pushing it uphill?

## 3. Customer Development

Treat the idea as hypotheses to test outside the building:

- customer hypothesis
- problem hypothesis
- value hypothesis
- channel hypothesis
- revenue hypothesis
- growth hypothesis

The output should identify the next milestone that proves or falsifies the riskiest hypothesis.

## 4. Mom Test interview quality

Good validation uses past behavior, current workaround, and real constraints.

Bad validation asks people to predict whether they would use or buy the idea.

## 5. Lean / Running Lean

Use a staged validation path:

1. Problem/segment fit: does the target customer have a painful problem?
2. Problem/solution fit: does the proposed workflow solve it enough to matter?
3. Product/market fit: do active users retain, refer, pay, or complain if it disappears?
4. Scale readiness: can distribution and unit economics work?

## 6. Value Proposition Canvas

Check fit between customer reality and offer:

- Jobs: functional, social, emotional jobs
- Pains: costs, risks, frustrations, blockers
- Gains: outcomes, status, speed, savings, upside
- Pain relievers: how the offer reduces pains
- Gain creators: how the offer creates gains

Weakness: the offer solves a pain the customer does not rank highly.

## 7. JTBD Forces of Progress

Switching happens when:

```text
Push of current pain + Pull of new solution > Habit of old way + Anxiety of switching
```

Score all four forces. Strong pain and strong product appeal still fail if habit/anxiety are higher.

## 8. PMF Engine / Sean Ellis lens

Use only when there are qualified active users.

Signals:

- large share of target active users would be very disappointed if the product disappeared
- disappointed users share a clear core benefit
- retention/repeat behavior supports the survey
- demand, support, or usage pressure exceeds current capacity
- organic referrals or word-of-mouth emerge in the target segment

Avoid claiming PMF from waitlists, investor interest, or one-time usage.

## 9. B2B validation lens

B2B scoring needs role clarity:

- user pain
- buyer value
- budget owner
- procurement/security path
- decision timeline
- current workflow cost
- paid pilot or design-partner path

Strong B2B evidence includes paid pilots, budgeted projects, data access, workflow artifacts, and executive/business owner urgency.

## 10. Consumer validation lens

Consumer ideas need evidence of:

- frequent use case or emotionally intense use case
- retention or repeat behavior
- share/referral motive
- acquisition channel and cost realism
- willingness to pay, attention, or data exchange

## 11. Marketplace validation lens

Marketplace ideas need:

- clear initial side to seed
- liquidity wedge
- supply/demand sequencing
- reason one side joins before the other is fully present
- take-rate and trust/safety assumptions

## 12. Deeptech or regulated validation lens

Deeptech, healthcare, fintech, education, and regulated ideas need:

- technical feasibility milestone
- regulatory path
- capital intensity estimate
- buyer/reimbursement path when relevant
- safety, privacy, or compliance constraints

## 13. AI-assisted validation guardrail

AI can help with:

- question design
- transcript clustering
- competitor and market desk research
- hypothesis generation
- draft experiment design

AI cannot replace:

- real customer behavior
- willingness to pay
- procurement friction
- retention
- lived customer context

Classify AI-generated personas and synthetic user responses as E0/E1 evidence at most.

## 14. Synthesis sequence

1. Frame idea, customer, current alternative, stage, and decision.
2. Extract riskiest hypotheses.
3. Build evidence inventory and tag E-levels.
4. Score Thiel 7Q for venture-scale potential.
5. Evaluate JTBD, VPC, PMF stage, and customer pull.
6. Apply confidence gates and false-positive penalties.
7. Choose verdict and next 7-day validation sprint.
