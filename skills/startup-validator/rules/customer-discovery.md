# Customer Discovery Rules

## Interview principle

Validate the customer’s life, behavior, and constraints. Do not ask the customer to judge your idea.

## Mom Test rules

| Rule | Ask | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Talk about their life | “지난번 이 문제를 어떻게 해결했나요?” | “우리 제품 쓸 건가요?” |
| Ask about specifics in the past | “마지막으로 이 작업을 한 날은 언제였나요?” | “보통 자주 하나요?” |
| Listen more than you pitch | follow-up on behavior and cost | five-minute product pitch |

Bad data:

- compliments
- hypotheticals
- feature wishlists
- vague averages
- “sounds interesting” without a next action

## Discovery output requirements

For each customer segment, capture:

- exact customer/persona and context
- current workaround or alternative
- trigger moment: when the pain becomes urgent
- frequency and severity
- time, money, risk, or status cost
- buying process or adoption blocker
- quotes from past behavior, not invented summary
- evidence level and confidence

## B2B discovery routing

Separate these roles:

- user: feels the workflow pain
- buyer: owns budget or procurement
- champion: wants the change internally
- blocker: can stop adoption
- economic buyer: signs or approves payment

Do not score B2B distribution highly until buyer, user, urgency, budget path, and buying timeline are at least provisionally mapped.

## SPIN-lite question set

Use when the user asks for B2B validation or the customer is an organization.

- Situation: “How does this workflow happen today?”
- Problem: “Where does it break, delay, or cost too much?”
- Implication: “What happens if this is not fixed?”
- Need-payoff: “What would change if this disappeared?”

## Pattern resonance

Strong discovery patterns include:

- multiple customers use the same urgent language without prompting
- customers already spend money or time on workarounds
- customers ask about timeline, price, pilot, data sharing, or procurement
- customers introduce peers with the same problem
- customers reveal a budget owner or existing line item

Weak patterns include:

- everyone is polite but nobody commits time, data, money, or introductions
- pain exists but is rare or low priority
- user pain exists but buyer value is unclear
- customers want a feature, not a product or workflow change

## Interview hygiene

- Write questions before calls.
- Ask fewer, better questions.
- Do not lead with the solution.
- Capture raw quotes and observed behavior.
- Separate interviewer interpretation from customer words.
- End by asking for the next concrete commitment only when the pain is real.
